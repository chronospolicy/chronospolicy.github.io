Temporal randomization

During training, we vary each modality’s observation rate and delivery delay, and randomize when the predicted action chunk begins. Selected measurements keep their original capture timestamps, so the policy can interpret each history even when its timing changes.

More training signal from the same demonstrations. Different resamplings can use measurements a fixed-rate grid would discard, giving the policy more views of each recorded trajectory and a wider range of timing conditions.

Real Robot Experiments

We vary observation frequency and delivery delay, inference latency, and their combination. Across Dynamic Cup, Peg Insertion, and Pivoting, Chronos Policy achieves the highest task score in every evaluated timing condition.

DP + RTC compensates for inference latency. Chronos Policy also represents observation capture times, extending robustness to changes in sensing.

Dynamic tasks: Dynamic Cup and Conveyor. Observation intervals let the policy infer object motion. Requested execution times tell it how far ahead to act when inference is delayed.

Multisensory tasks: Peg Insertion and Pivoting. Vision, proprioception, and force/torque measurements are acquired at different rates and times. Timestamps preserve their relative timing when sequence positions no longer reliably indicate when they were acquired.

When time matters most

Conveyor and Pivoting test these two timing relationships in simulation. The comparisons show all five policy variants under nominal and changed sensing and inference timing.

Ablations

Why timestamps and temporal randomization work together

Temporal randomization exposes a policy to different schedules, but without timestamps the policy cannot tell which schedule produced its inputs. Additive time encoding supplies timestamps through token features. Time-aware RoPE instead makes elapsed time part of attention while preserving token content.

The ablations compare both ingredients directly. Randomization alone helps Conveyor under moderate shifts but struggles under larger changes and on Pivoting. RoPE without randomization also loses robustness. Chronos Policy combines time-aware RoPE with temporal randomization.

Chronos Policy preserves performance when timing is less critical

Across six DexMimicGen tasks, Chronos Policy achieves comparable nominal performance to Diffusion Policy and higher average success when observation timing or inference latency changes.
